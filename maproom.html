LOCAL AI · GOVERNANCE · SECURITY RESEARCH

Maproom

An ongoing investigation into whether local AI workers can perform bounded tasks, preserve evidence, undergo independent review, and improve over time without controlling their own security boundaries.

01 / WorkSmall, inspectable assignments instead of all-at-once prompts.
02 / EvidenceTraceable outputs, tests, decisions, and operational telemetry.
03 / AuthorityPermissions established by trusted infrastructure, not a model's assertions.

The research question: Can a collection of specialized AI workers perform useful work and progressively improve while external systems retain control of permissions, approvals, and deployment?

EXPERIMENT TO DATE

Django, one component at a time

A locally hosted Qwen model was used in a modular Django development exercise, producing foundational configuration and elements of landing and registration functionality. The result was not a complete production application.

Breaking large assignments into bounded pieces helped make generated work easier to inspect and carry forward without repeatedly sending an entire project into model context. Quantitative improvements have not yet been published.

SECURITY FOUNDATION

Segmentation and observation

The lab uses Linux services, Proxmox, managed VLANs, OPNsense firewall controls, PostgreSQL, and Grafana/Loki observability. Qwen runs locally, and the AI environment is designed around restricted communication paths.

Network segmentation, monitoring, and model refusal behavior are not substitutes for application-layer authorization. Exact private topology and operational details are intentionally omitted.

WORKFLOW RESEARCH

Builder → Reviewer → Manager → Steward

The research pipeline separates work generation, assessment, next-step decisions, and governance. An important design requirement is preserving the original artifact and review evidence across each handoff.

The complete autonomous lifecycle should not be interpreted as fully deployed or independently audited.

UNDER DEVELOPMENT

Trainer and trusted controller

The proposed Trainer will diagnose recurring failure modes and support bounded improvement campaigns, including scheduled evaluation of the Manager. A separate trusted controller is intended to verify authority before approved changes reach application code or infrastructure.

Centralized identity services such as FreeIPA and API identities are possible future extensions, not existing deployed controls.

WHAT THE EXPERIMENT TEACHES

Govern the process, not just the prompt

  • Bounded tasks can reduce the amount of change that must be reviewed, but scope is only enforceable when the tools and execution environment honor it.
  • Artifact hashes, logs, and evaluations can establish traceability; they do not independently prove that an output is safe.
  • A model's ability to propose an action does not confer permission to perform it.
  • Training is an evidence-driven decision, not the automatic response to every failed task.
